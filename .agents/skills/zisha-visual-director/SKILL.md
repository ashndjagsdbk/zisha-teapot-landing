# Zisha Visual Director

## Goal

Turn a zisha teapot website into a premium, image-led, immersive product experience.

The page should feel like a scrollable product commercial, not a normal corporate website or Taobao detail page.

## Visual Thesis

Dark, warm, restrained, tactile, and cinematic.

The teapot is the protagonist. Clay, fire, water, handwork, and time are the supporting elements.

## Required Visual Language

Use:
- dark brown / black clay backgrounds
- narrow light photography
- zisha clay texture
- macro material details
- amber tea light
- kiln-fire undertones
- large negative space
- strong product scale
- restrained typography
- slow scroll rhythm
- sparse copy
- cinematic section composition

Avoid:
- generic SaaS card grids
- cheap e-commerce layout
- large white background
- colorful gradients
- cartoon icons
- noisy Chinese traditional decoration
- overused words like 匠心, 非遗, 传承 unless supported by concrete details
- floating card piles
- excessive shadows
- meaningless animation

## Page Structure

Preferred structure:

1. Hero
- full-screen visual
- teapot occupies the visual center
- one strong headline
- one short subtitle
- two CTA buttons

2. Clay Story
- full-screen clay macro image
- minimal text
- explain clay as material, not mythology

3. Teapot Explorer
- fixed teapot visual
- clickable parts: spout, handle, lid, body, inner wall, bottom seal
- each part has one image and one concise explanation

4. Craft Process
- cinematic horizontal sequence
- one step per screen
- use image-led craft storytelling

5. Patina / Time
- show change from new teapot to long-term use
- emphasize time, touch, tea, and surface glow

6. Trust
- display clay, capacity, craft, author, certificate, packaging, care
- make it feel like an art object specification label

7. Final CTA
- dark cinematic closing
- guide users to consult, reserve, or customize

## Motion Rules

Use motion only for hierarchy and atmosphere.

Good:
- slow hero entrance
- subtle scroll-linked scale
- sticky storytelling
- image reveal
- section fade and lift
- hotspot transitions

Bad:
- bouncing elements
- random rotation
- excessive parallax
- colorful motion
- animation that makes reading harder

## Typography

Use few type sizes, strong contrast, and large spacing.

Headlines should feel editorial and premium.
Body copy should be short.
If deleting 30% of the copy improves the page, delete it.

## Quality Checklist

Before finishing, check:

- Is the teapot unmistakably the protagonist?
- Does the first screen feel like a poster?
- Does every section have one visual idea?
- Are cards reduced to the minimum?
- Is the page still premium without decorative shadows?
- Is the copy restrained?
- Is the mobile version still cinematic?
- Does the CTA remain clear but not cheap?
